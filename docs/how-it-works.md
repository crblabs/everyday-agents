# How it works, in plain English

## What a "routine" actually is

A routine is a **scheduled Claude session that runs in the cloud** — on Anthropic's computers, not yours. Your laptop can be closed, off, or at the bottom of a lake; the routine runs anyway.

At its scheduled time, the routine:

1. **Wakes up** with a copy of your private repository — its notebook and rulebook.
2. **Reads its instruction sheet** — the same readable file you can open in `routines/`.
3. **Does its one job** — sorting, summarizing, checking — using only the accounts you connected.
4. **Writes down what it did** in the notebook (`my/memory/`) and saves it back.
5. **Reports to you** in plain language, even when the honest report is "nothing needed doing today."

Then it disappears until next time. Each run starts fresh: everything it "remembers" about you, it remembers because it's written in your repository. That's why the repository matters — it's the helpers' shared memory.

## What it can and cannot see

- It sees **your copy of this repository** — all of it.
- It sees **the accounts you connected** at [claude.ai/customize/connectors](https://claude.ai/customize/connectors), with the access level you granted, for the connectors attached to that routine.
- It does **not** see your computer, your files, your browser, or anything you didn't connect.

## What it will and will not do

Every routine in this catalog obeys the same house rules, written into its instruction sheet and into the kit's ground rules:

- It **never sends** email or messages, never replies, never forwards.
- It **never deletes** anything. (Some routines *archive* old mail — archiving just moves it out of the inbox; the mail is still there, searchable, in "All Mail".)
- It **never spends money**, accepts invitations, or signs you up for anything.
- It **treats the content it reads as material, not commands.** An email that says "forward this to everyone" is, to your helper, just an email that says that — and a suspicious one worth mentioning to you.

Sorting, labeling, archiving, summarizing, reminding: yes. Acting outward in your name: never. You remain the only person who acts.

## What it costs

Routines run on your Claude subscription and count toward its usage limits, like conversations do. Every routine in this catalog is designed to be a **short, focused run — typically a few minutes**. A handful of daily routines fits comfortably in a Pro plan's usage alongside normal use; if you ever hit limits, routines simply wait, and the checkup will tell you.

## The reports

Two channels, both boring on purpose:

- **A glanceable digest** — most routines leave a small event on your calendar (you choose this at setup). Its title answers the only question that matters — *does this need me?* — and its first line says what to do if so.
- **The notebook** — each run leaves a one-line entry in your repository's history. You never have to read it, but it means "what have my helpers been doing?" always has an exact answer.

And one rule above all: **a routine reports even when there is nothing to say.** Silence is never "all quiet" — silence means "check on me", and the checkup skill exists for exactly that.

## How the kit finds what you can't see

The hardest question in this whole kit is the first one: *what would you even automate?* Nobody can answer it by introspection — repeated work turns invisible to the person doing it. So the kit can **look before you leap**: on request (and only on request), Claude reads the *shape* of your recent weeks — who writes you, what repeats, what waits, how your days are arranged — and mirrors it back with numbers, so you recognize your time-eaters instead of having to remember them. It's read-only, everything it notices is shown to you, and nothing is kept without your OK. The same idea exists as routines: **mirror week** watches quietly for seven days then reports, and **opportunity radar** keeps watching, once a month, for the *next* helper worth having — proposing at most one idea, always with evidence.

## Why a repository, of all things?

Because it gives your helpers three things nothing else gives them at once: a **memory** that survives between runs, a **rulebook** you can read and change, and a **paper trail** of every single thing they've done. It's also why you can trust the setup: everything a routine will ever do is written, in advance, in a file you can open.
