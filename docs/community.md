# The community, in plain English

Everyone who uses this kit has their own private copy — but the kit itself is shared, and it grows by what people give back. This page explains how that works and, more importantly, how little you need to do to take part: **everything happens by talking to Claude.**

## What you can say

- **"What's new in the kit?"** — see routines and packs the community added since your copy was made, and install any you like. Your copy never goes stale just because you made it a while ago.
- **"What are people wishing for?"** — browse the community's open wishes. If one matches something you've built (or want to build), Claude will bridge you straight into building or sharing it.
- **"Share my … routine"** — offer a routine you built to the community. Claude scrubs it of everything personal first and shows you exactly what it removed; nothing goes anywhere until you've seen the final text and said yes.
- **"I wish a helper could …"** — file a wish. You don't need to know how to build it; wishes are how the catalog learns what's missing, and someone else may pick it up.
- **"This routine keeps doing X wrong"** / **"I got lost at step 3"** — send a field report or a confusion report. Both genuinely improve the kit for the next person; confusion is a contribution here, not a failure.

## The promises, short version

Sharing is **opt-in, previewed, and scrubbed** — personal details are replaced before anything leaves your copy, and you see the removals. Every gift is **credited** the way you choose (or anonymous). And **a human reads every line** of a shared routine — against a [public checklist](../CONTRIBUTING.md) — before it becomes installable by anyone else. When you install a community routine, Claude tells you out loud which shelf it came from: the **core** set (written with the kit), the **community** shelf (contributed, human-reviewed), or neither — in which case it won't install it.

## What happens behind the curtain

One honest paragraph for the curious: the kit lives on GitHub, and community traffic travels as GitHub "issues" — little public notes on the kit's page. When you share or wish, Claude either posts the note for you or hands you a link where the form is already filled in and one green button submits it. When you browse, Claude reads the kit's public files and notes directly — no account needed. You never have to touch any of this machinery yourself; but it's all public and inspectable, which is exactly why it can be trusted.

## One thing to know

Wishes, reports, and shared routines are **public** once submitted — that's what makes them useful to everyone. Claude scrubs personal details before anything goes out, and shows you the text first, so what's public is only ever what you approved.
