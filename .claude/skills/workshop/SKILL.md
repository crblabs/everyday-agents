# The opportunity workshop — from "no idea" to a running helper, in one sitting

This is the kit's ceremonial front door: the discovery experience run *properly*, end to end, for someone giving it a real hour. Method adapted from Bornet et al., *Agentic Artificial Intelligence* (their task-inventory → impact → feasibility → effort → quick-wins method) — with one deliberate inversion: **the inventory is done by evidence and recognition, never by asking the participant to list their tasks**, because nobody can list their own invisible work.

A group in the room? Each participant runs the workshop in their *own* copy with their own Claude — this conversation serves one person; say so warmly if several are implied.

## Movement 1 — Two minutes of vocabulary (cure capability-blindness first)

People can't spot opportunities for a tool they can't picture. Before anything, give the two-minute version, in kit voice, no jargon:

- What a helper is: a small scheduled task that runs in the cloud, reads only what you've connected, and reports in plain words.
- What helpers are good at: *watching, sorting, summarizing, reminding, preparing* — the gestures you repeat.
- What they never do here: send, delete, spend, decide for you. You stay the one who acts.
- The punchline that unlocks people: **"the best candidates are things you've stopped noticing you do."**

Keep it to two minutes. The workshop is about their life, not about AI.

## Movement 2 — The inventory (run the discover skill's engine)

Follow the **discover** skill's procedure — it is the engine of this movement; don't duplicate it, run it: the consent gate verbatim, then Path A (mirror their last 2–4 weeks of connected email/calendar back to them, shape and numbers, never content) or Path B (the moments deck from `routines/MOMENTS.md`), or both when the mirror is thin. The workshop difference is pacing: linger here. Let them react to each pattern; collect every sting into a named candidate list *in their words* ("the school flood", "the receipt pile"). Aim for 5–8 candidates before moving on — this movement is the workshop.

## Movement 3 — The map (triage silently, present the quadrants)

Score every candidate in your head — impact / feasibility / effort, per the discover skill's triage — then lay out their **personal map**, in plain words, three shelves:

1. **Quick wins** (2–3): would pay back within the month; each mapped to its answer (catalog routine or build).
2. **One project worth wanting**: bigger, real; name what makes it bigger and what would have to be true first.
3. **The honest not-yets**: each with its reason ("the data lives where no connection reaches", "every rule came out as 'it depends'") — and the offer to file the best one as a community wish. This shelf is what makes the other two believable.

No grids, no scores, no framework names — shelves and sentences.

## Movement 4 — Leave with a running helper

The workshop's contract: they walk out with their first quick win **scheduled, not listed**. Hand the top pick to **add-routine** (catalog) or **build-routine** (custom) and see it through — connections, personalization, schedule, the lot. Then:

- Write their map to `my/memory/workshop-YYYY-MM-DD.md`: the candidates in their words, the three shelves, what was scheduled. Commit (`workshop YYYY-MM-DD: mapped N candidates, scheduled <name>`), push.
- Close the loop out loud: when the first report will arrive and where; the standing phrases ("what should I automate" re-runs discovery anytime, "check my routines", "what's new"); and — if a not-yet stung — that the wish is filed and the community sometimes grants wishes.

One rule for the whole hour: their words beat the kit's. If they call it "the Sunday dread", its helper gets presented as the cure for the Sunday dread — not as "week ahead".
